## The Physical Framework

### What a Void Actually Is

A cosmic void is not merely an underdensity in the galaxy distribution. It is the **geometric center of a formerly gravitationally bound system that dark energy tore apart.**

At early times (z~2-5), what is now a void was a region of slightly below-average density — a proto-underdensity embedded within a larger proto-supercluster system. The surrounding mass was gravitationally bound or marginally bound to this system. As dark energy became dominant (z<1), the expansion rate increased. Structures that were marginally bound began to unbind. Dark energy drove clusters past their mutual escape velocities. The proto-system fragmented:

- **The void center** became the geometric center of a diverging system — not a gravitational attractor, but the **null point** of a broken gravitational bond, analogous to the center of an explosion where expansion is maximum
- **The surrounding supercluster filaments** became the escaped fragments — carrying outward peculiar velocity vectors that are the fossil kinematic record of the unbinding event
- **The void boundary** (turnaround radius) marks the scale where dark energy overcame gravity — where infall toward surrounding walls transitions to outflow from the void center

This reframing is observationally confirmed: Nasonova & Karachentsev measure ~300 km/s outflow amplitude from 1056 galaxies inside the Local Void. Our PSZ2 test (June 13, 2026) found 7/7 nearby clusters showing net outflow (v_pec > 0), t=7.3, p=0.0003 — though at attenuated amplitude (14.1 km/s at r~65 Mpc vs 155 km/s expected from linear theory, consistent with PSZ2 incompleteness at z<0.02).

### The Velocity Vector Signature

If surrounding supercluster clusters are escaped fragments of a broken proto-system, their peculiar velocity vectors should point **radially outward from the void center**, with amplitude decreasing with distance as:

v_outflow(r) ≈ v_max × (R_void / r)    for r > R_void

This is the escape velocity profile of an unbound system. The turnaround radius — where v_outflow transitions to v_infall toward surrounding walls — encodes the local dark energy equation of state. In the equilibrium framework, this radius should be **larger** around voids with more massive surrounding supercluster systems (dark energy locally suppressed at supercluster nodes, pulling the turnaround outward) and **smaller** around isolated voids (dark energy locally enhanced, turnaround compressed inward).

### The Equilibrium Field

Dark energy is not spatially uniform. It behaves as a **locally equilibrated field** — when it drives anomalous expansion in one region (void), it is suppressed in adjacent regions (superclusters), allowing gravity to locally dominate and deepen potential wells. The field maintains conservation-like equilibrium across both space and cosmic time.

**Standard ΛCDM ISW (expected):**
- Potentials decay uniformly everywhere
- Overdensities: decaying well → photon exits shallower → warm CMB
- Voids: decaying hill → photon exits shallower → cold CMB

**Dark Energy Equilibrium Field (observed):**
- DE locally enhanced at void centers → anomalous expansion → potential shallows faster than ΛCDM → photon through void exits much shallower hill → **anomalously warm void**
- DE locally suppressed at supercluster nodes → gravity dominates → potential deepens during photon transit → photon exits deeper well than entered → **anomalously cold overdensity**
- Both at amplitudes exceeding ΛCDM by ~10×, because this is non-linear field structure rather than smooth linear decay

### Temporal Non-Locality

The field is not confined to a single epoch. Its equilibrium operates across cosmic time:

- **z~0.12 (lookback ~1.6 Gyr):** Dark energy suppressed in massive supercluster regions → mergers proceed unimpeded, clusters disturbed, potentials deepen → cold ISW at disturbed clusters. This is our key bin signal.
- **z~0.05 (lookback ~680 Myr):** Shapley supercluster — the most massive attractor in our neighborhood, visible in our past lightcone at 640 Myr lookback — dark energy still suppressed there → cold ISW. Our measurement: T = −10.66 μK. Note: Shapley is in our past, not our present — we observe it as it was 640 Myr ago.
- **z~0.03 (lookback ~400 Myr):** Dark energy reasserting as supercluster assembly completes → compensating expansion in adjacent voids → warm ISW at void centers. This is F.H.'s anomaly.
- **z~0 (now):** We feel the equilibrium dynamically — peculiar velocity away from Local Void (~259 km/s), toward Great Attractor/Shapley (~455 km/s). The CMB dipole encodes both the kinematic and ISW contributions of this system.

These are not independent epochs sampling a uniform ISW kernel. They are **temporally coupled phases** of one equilibrating field. The cold disturbed clusters at z~0.12 and the hot voids at z~0.03 are the same process observed at different moments along its evolution.

### The Local Group as a Boundary Observer

We sit at the edge of the Local Void, adjacent to Laniakea. This is the optimal geometric position to observe the equilibrium:

- **Behind us — Local Void:** DE field at local maximum, driving anomalous expansion, surrounding clusters moving outward as escaped fragments. CMB temperature: +22.25 μK warm (our measurement).
- **Ahead of us — Great Attractor, Shapley (in past lightcone):** DE field suppressed, gravity dominant, potentials deepening. CMB temperature: −10.66 μK cold (Shapley, our measurement).
- **Our peculiar velocity:** The dynamical fingerprint of the equilibrium. We are being pushed by the void's dark energy enhancement and pulled by the supercluster's gravitational deepening simultaneously.
- **CMB dipole:** Partially kinematic, but the anomalous ISW component from the void-attractor equilibrium contributes to what we interpret as a pure Doppler signal. The Dipole Repeller (Hoffman et al. 2017) — the underdensity contributing ~50% of our peculiar velocity — is in this framework the region of maximum local DE enhancement, producing the strongest warm ISW in our neighborhood.

## Observational Tests — June 13, 2026

### Test 1: PSZ2 Pipeline Correction and Null Results

Correct compensated aperture (30' disk minus 30'–45' annulus background) applied. Original 2.84σ binary Y-M residual split does not survive:
- Continuous correlation: r=0.030, p=0.76 — null
- Key bin mean: 0.93σ — not significant
- Wavelet: 1.36σ — not significant

Y-M residual is not a reliable morphology classifier (F.H. critique confirmed via eROSITA cross-match).

### Test 2: SZ Contamination

Compared SMICA no-SZ vs SMICA+SZ for 108 key bin clusters. SZ contribution: 1.69 μK — negligible. Signal is not SZ contamination.

### Test 3: eROSITA + Sanders 2025 D-comb

996 clusters at z=[0.10,0.15) with real X-ray morphology. Full sample: −3.22 μK, 1.15σ — null. PSZ2 mass range: disturbed WARMER (+8.17 μK) — opposite sign to PSZ2 Y-M residual. Confirms merger stage clock: different classifiers select different merger phases with physically predicted opposite ISW signs.

### Test 4: Within-Supercluster Pair Analysis

CMB temperature differential between disturbed and nearest relaxed cluster within <60 Mpc (supercluster scale):
- n=12 pairs, mean differential: **−28.45 μK, 2.34σ**
- Sign consistency: **10/12 cold, binomial p=0.039**

Strongest surviving PSZ2 signal. Within-pair method cancels shared primordial CMB, isolates ISW at supercluster scale.

### Test 5: Supercluster Multiplicity Gradient

Disturbed-minus-relaxed CMB differential by supercluster richness in eROSITA key bin:

| Multiplicity | Mean dis-rel ΔT |
|-------------|----------------|
| 2–3 members | +0.83 μK |
| 3–4 members | +4.02 μK |
| 4–6 members | −8.41 μK |
| 6–50 members | **−13.17 μK** |

Monotonic cold gradient with richness. Richer supercluster systems surround larger voids with stronger mass flow — disturbed clusters at void-SC boundaries show deepest potential evolution → coldest ISW. Not statistically significant (0.95σ) due to small per-SC sample counts, but direction is the predicted one.

## Unified Physical Picture

### The Void-Supercluster System as Dark Energy Detector

The void-supercluster system is not two independent structures. It is **one gravitational system at different stages of dark energy breakup:**

```
Early universe (z>1):
  Proto-supercluster system — bound, collapsing, dark energy subdominant
  
Dark energy breakup (z~0.5-1):
  Marginally bound members escape → void forms at former center of mass
  Surrounding clusters carry outward velocity vectors (fossil record)
  
Recent universe (z<0.1):
  Void expanding anomalously fast (DE locally enhanced at null point)
  Surrounding SCs deepening (DE locally suppressed, gravity winning)
  CMB encodes both: hot void center, cold SC nodes
  
Now (z=0):
  We feel it as bulk flow — pushed by void DE, pulled by SC gravity
  CMB dipole carries both kinematic and anomalous ISW contributions
```

### Why the Amplitude Exceeds ΛCDM by 10×

Standard ISW uses the linear growth factor — dark energy produces smooth, gradual potential decay at rates set by the background equation of state. The equilibrium field produces **locally structured, non-linear potential evolution.** Regions where dark energy is suppressed can have potentials deepening at rates far exceeding the linear prediction. The 10× excess amplitude is not a violation of physics — it is the signature of a spatially structured dark energy field operating in the non-linear regime of large-scale structure, analogous to how non-linear structure growth produces the Rees-Sciama effect at cluster scales.

### Field Behavior Across Redshift

The equilibrium field is not temporally parochial. It does not operate only at a specific redshift. Rather:

- At z>0.3: dark energy subdominant, gravity wins everywhere, standard ISW tiny, equilibrium effect negligible
- At z~0.1-0.2: dark energy becoming significant, first equilibrium structures forming, disturbed clusters at proto-void boundaries show cold signal (our key bin)
- At z~0.03-0.1: equilibrium fully established, voids expanding anomalously, SCs cold, F.H.'s anomaly range
- At z~0: field at maximum non-linearity, our peculiar velocity, CMB dipole, Dipole Repeller all manifestations of one equilibrated system

The field has no preferred redshift — it is active wherever dark energy is locally competing with gravity. But it is MEASURABLE only at low z where the equilibrium has had time to develop and where individual void-SC systems can be spatially resolved.
